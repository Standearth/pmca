We Canvassed at Whole Foods — And We’re Just Getting Started

"We are organized, we are vocal, and we want to see better from Amazon."

I'll be honest — I almost didn't go. Standing in a parking lot and approaching strangers isn't exactly in my comfort zone. But I kept thinking: Amazon Prime members are one of the largest consumer groups on the planet. If we don't speak up, who will?

So my friend grabbed her phone for photos, I tucked a stack of handouts under my arm and pocketed a handful of stickers, and headed to our local Whole Foods. Within 10 minutes, we were having real conversations with real people — right in front of that iconic storefront sign.

Why Whole Foods?

Amazon’s transportation emissions grew 75% between 2019 and 2023 — just for dock-to-door deliveries in the U.S. Meanwhile, recent Morning Consult polling found that 4 in 5 Prime members want Amazon to take stronger action on emissions. We’re not outliers. We’re the majority.

It's simple: Amazon owns it. Every customer walking out those doors is part of the Amazon ecosystem — and some of them didn't know it. That moment of mild surprise ("Wait, Amazon owns this place?") is exactly the kind of opening we need. It's a conversation-starter, not a confrontation.

Standing near the main entrance, with the Whole Foods sign clearly visible behind us, we became part of something visible and physical. This isn't just an online petition. We're out here, in person, making the case that Amazon Prime members care about the climate. And we’re not alone — Prime members are doing this at Whole Foods locations across the country.

What we brought

Our kit was simple: handouts explaining what Prime Members for a Cleaner Amazon is and why we're organizing, and stickers — because everyone, it turns out, loves a sticker. We also brought the two things you can't pack in a bag: determination and a willingness to get a little uncomfortable.

From the Canvassing Kit

You need two people: one to approach shoppers, one to take photos and video. The documentation, the proof that we showed up, matters as much as the conversations themselves.

How the conversations actually went

Most interactions were short — 10 seconds, maybe 15. Someone walks out, we make eye contact, offer a sticker. Some people smiled and kept walking. Some stopped. A few asked questions. And some of those conversations turned into something genuinely meaningful.

The script that worked best for us? Disarmingly simple:

Example interaction that worked

→"Hi! Good morning!"

→"Here's a sticker for you." (offer the sticker — it breaks the ice every time)

→"We're Prime Members for a Cleaner Amazon — we're asking Amazon to electrify its delivery fleet, decarbonize data centres, and create better working conditions."

→"Scan the QR code if you'd like to join us. Have a good one!"

The trick is staying friendly and keeping it moving. The goal isn't to win a debate — it's to plant a seed and, where possible, get a photo that shows the world we're out there.

What I didn't expect

I didn't expect how many people already had opinions. Several shoppers mentioned Amazon's delivery trucks without any prompting from us. One woman said she'd been thinking about quitting Prime because of exactly this issue. Another asked if we had a website.

We also got some polite no-thank-yous, a few averted gazes, and one person who really, genuinely did not want to talk to us. That's fine. The kit says it best: focus your energy on the few who do engage, not the many who don't. Canvassing is a learned skill, and every "no" is just practice.

Mindset shift that helped

Stop thinking of every interaction as a potential win or loss. Think of it as planting seeds. Most seeds don't sprout right in front of you. That's okay. Keep planting.

The photos matter

Here's something I underestimated before we went out: the photos are part of the activism. Each image of a Prime member, standing outside a Whole Foods, holding our handout or wearing our sticker, contributes to a growing national narrative. We look organized because we are. We look like a movement because we're becoming one.

We made sure the Whole Foods sign was in the background. We made sure every person we photographed consented. And we walked away with a handful of images that tell the story better than any press release could.

You should do this too

I don't say that lightly. I know it's awkward to approach strangers. I know it takes time on a weekend morning when you could be doing literally anything else. But this is how movements grow — not through algorithms, but through humans showing up in physical space and talking to each other.

You don't need to be a seasoned organizer. You need two people, some handouts, some stickers, and the willingness to be a little uncomfortable for about an hour. If I can do it, you can do it.

There are more of us today than there were yesterday – 25,000 Prime members and counting in dozens of cities across the country.
